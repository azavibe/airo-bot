# Give Airobot's bots memory, receipts and reach

Users of both the hyperscaler agent platforms and the small-business "AI employee" products reward the same three things: a bot that already knows their business and data, work that arrives as a draft they approve, and a short proactive brief delivered where they already look. They punish three things too: credits that drain unpredictably, output that is generic or falsely "done", and bots that lose context between chats. Airobot already has a skeleton that answers much of this, with one office chat where the Architect routes work to area bots, approvals, a budget gate, a daily plan, Run/Stop, a local mode that uses the owner's own AI tools with no credits, and self-hosting. But its bots forget and cannot see. A chat reply sees only the last 20 messages of one room, the lessons the owner teaches are never read in chat, the kickoff summary of the business scrolls out of view, and the bots in the chat cannot call the Google, Stripe or Shopify connections the owner set up. The work that pays off most is therefore connecting parts that already exist: put a stored business profile and the lessons into every reply, let chat bots read connected tools in draft-only mode, show honest task outcomes and per-bot spend, and fix a bug that hides the newest messages when a long room is reloaded. Three new data models come next, and all of them fit stock PostgreSQL 16 using the `citext` extension Airobot already enables plus built-in full-text search: contacts keyed on email where bots suggest edits rather than overwrite, a visible four-scope memory list with a rolling summary per room, and a document library whose citations are built from stored chunk ids, with pgvector left for later. "Kiro Crew" is almost certainly AWS's open-source Kiro Crew, whose persistent Bots reached general availability on 5 November 2026; its goal, milestone, check-in and report cards, editable memory, permission modes and log-backed dashboards map onto modules Airobot already has, though Airobot should not copy its trust-by-default setting. Treat the user-sentiment evidence as a pointer rather than proof, because Reddit was unreachable, most review text was read only as search snippets, and many "reviews" come from competitors or affiliates.

## Users reward bots that know the business and punish surprise bills

At the hyperscalers, reviewers praise integration with data they already hold more than raw intelligence. Gartner reviewers of Copilot Studio (about 4.3/5 from roughly 90 ratings) like its fit with the Microsoft ecosystem and how easy it is to build an agent ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/microsoft-copilot-studio)), and Amazon Q Business averages **4.4 from 270 Gartner ratings** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/generative-ai-apps/vendor/amazon-web-services/product/amazon-q-business)). The warmest praise goes to narrow agents with a number attached: Intercom Fin reviewers report "resolving 75% of tickets" and a "70%+ resolution rate" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/fin/reviews)). In the small-business category, praise is about speed and channel. Sintra's app-store reviewers say it is like having "12 staff" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sintra-ai-employees/id6737126864?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)), and ease of use comes up in **125 of 171** Lindy G2 reviews ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/lindy-ai-review/)). Lindy relaunched in February 2026 as an assistant you text over iMessage or SMS, complete with a 7 AM briefing, and one reviewer says this makes it "feel like an EA rather than software" ([mejba.me](https://www.mejba.me/blog/lindy-ai-executive-assistant-review)). Marblism includes every "employee" for a flat $24–44 a month, which earns it "the best dollar-for-dollar deal in the AI employee category" ([pondero.ai](https://pondero.ai/agents/reviews/marblism-review-may-2026)).

The loudest complaint, by a wide margin, is the usage meter. Sintra reviewers say "you run out of credits in like one heavy day of use" and "they even charge credits for just talking to the AI" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/sintra.ai)). A Lindy reviewer "managed to kill my months credits (3000) in one day!", and another says a request costs "2 credits or 2,000, and you learn which after it runs" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lindy.ai); [getmacha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/lindy-ai-complete-guide)). Manus users report spending "$100 in credits just to go in circles" ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/manus-ai-review/)). The enterprise products show the same pattern: HubSpot credits auto-replenish by default ([Pluno](https://pluno.ai/blog/hubspot-breeze-ai)), and Fin roundups cite bills jumping "from ~$119 to $854/mo" ([AI Support Crew](https://aisupportcrew.com/guides/intercom-fin-review-2026)). Billing friction makes it worse. Lindy reviewers report no cancel button and charges made "without any clear warning or confirmation step" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lindy.ai)), and one Marblism user found the agents still running after cancelling and called it "Zombie AI" ([Trustpilot AU](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/www.marblism.com?page=6)). This explains a striking split in ratings: Lindy scores **4.9 on G2 but under 2 on Trustpilot**, where nearly all the negative reviews are about billing ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/lindy-ai-review/)).

Quality complaints share one shape: the output is generic, wrong or falsely complete, and the user cannot see why. A HubSpot user quoted by a consultancy says "I don't trust it to send emails or update records" ([Process Pro](https://www.processproconsulting.com/resources/breeze-ai-what-works-what-needs-improvement-what-hubspot-users-are-saying)). The Breeze customer agent "would recommend things outside of the knowledge base or try to make things up" ([Warmly](https://www.warmly.ai/p/blog/breeze-intelligence-review)), and Copilot Studio reviewers say "accuracy of outputs requires additional manual verifications" ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/microsoft-copilot-studio)). Sintra users who skip its Brain AI setup "get generic output that is not much better than a general AI chatbot" ([roborhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/sintra-ai-review/)), and Manus reported a broken website as "fully completed" ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/manus-ai-review/)). Users name lost context directly: a Sintra review says "helpers don't share context" ([sistava](https://sistava.com/en/ai-agent-platform-reviews/ai-workforce/sintra-ai)), and a Viktor user found it "doing work in one Slack channel and had no idea what it was doing in another" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/viktor-com/reviews)). Salesforce drew the company-wide conclusion in December 2025, moving Agentforce toward LLM reasoning plus deterministic execution because models ignored specific instructions ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/is-salesforce-losing-confidence-in-llms/)). Users also resent AI pushed into places they did not ask for it. Salesforce replaced its Help search with an agent, users found the agent slower and more error-prone, and search came back on 14 November 2025 ([CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/ai/salesforce-axes-search-in-favor-of-agentforce-users-push-back/); [Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/trailblazer-feedback-forces-salesforce-to-revert-agentforce-support-page/)).

What users ask for is mostly control and transparency, not more capability. The requests that recur are one shared business memory every bot uses, and drafts before anything goes out; in Marblism "nothing goes live until you publish or schedule it" ([Marblism help](https://help.marblism.com/en/articles/17217749-getting-started-with-marblism)). Practitioners want approval only when an action crosses a risk threshold ([Ask HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46719774)), and a Zapier user asked for a "Completed, No Action" status instead of a run that looks stuck ([Zapier Community](https://community.zapier.com/troubleshooting-99/run-agent-behavior-step-delayed-stuck-49410)). Others want daily briefings in a channel they already read, small top-ups instead of forced plan upgrades ([Trustpilot CA](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/sintra.ai?page=2)), human handoffs that never dead-end ([HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/t/how-can-i-limit-customer-agent-questions-per-user-in-hubspot/139744)), and a choice of model ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/agentspace/reviews)). Vendors have shipped the same answers. Copilot Studio's human-in-the-loop pauses an agent and sends the reviewer an Outlook form ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/blog/copilot-studio/whats-new-in-microsoft-copilot-studio-november-2025/)), Salesforce logs every planner decision in a session trace ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/agentforce-observability/)), and HubSpot added memory, file upload and connections to Breeze Assistant ([CX Today](https://cxtoday.com/event-news/hubspot-inbound-2025-the-top-10-announcements/)) along with a Sources section showing what it referenced ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/ai/use-breeze-assistant?gn=1)). The category is also stepping back from autonomy: Artisan retired its "Stop Hiring Humans" campaign and hired its first human sales rep in August 2026 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/artisan-told-companies-to-stop-hiring-humans-for-sales-now-its-hiring-one-for-just-that-job)). The market is moving toward a supervised helper that does admin work, which is what Airobot already promises.

## Airobot has the right skeleton, but its bots forget and cannot see

Airobot's design already answers several of the loudest complaints. There is one office group chat, where the Architect either answers or hands out up to three `@handle: task` lines and an @mention goes straight to the named bot ([group.rs](../src/office/group.rs)); that is a structural answer to "helpers don't share context", at least within that chat. Nothing goes out without a person, because Gmail is draft-only, Calendar sends no invites, and Stripe and Shopify are read-only ([connectors.rs](../src/connectors.rs)). The meter is legible: the budget gate charges a flat **25 credits per AI call**, caps a day at 5,000 and sends anything above 200 credits to an approval ([budget_gate.rs](../src/office/budget_gate.rs)). If the model call behind a chat reply fails the credits are refunded, and when a limit is hit the bot posts a plain-language note ([threads.rs](../src/handlers/threads.rs)). Run/Stop halts replies, kickoff tasks and daily plans for an office ([0052_office_pause.sql](../migrations/0052_office_pause.sql)), and the kickoff plans are fixed text that costs nothing until work starts ([kickoff.rs](../src/office/kickoff.rs)). Each bot can use its own model, local mode runs on the owner's own AI tools with no metering ([local.rs](../src/local.rs)), and bots speak when asked; the only scheduled push, the 08:00 daily plan, can be turned off ([daily.rs](../src/office/daily.rs)).

The gaps are in what bots know and can see. A chat reply's history is the **last 20 notes of that one thread** (`conversation_history(&pool, thread_id, 20)` at line 1597 of [threads.rs](../src/handlers/threads.rs)), plus the bot's one-sentence character, the office's name, type, website and monthly focus, and the app schemas without their records ([context.rs](../src/office/context.rs)). The kickoff's summary of the business website is posted as a chat message, so it drops out of every bot's view after about 20 messages. Lessons are captured when the owner rejects an approval or gives a reason ([airo.rs](../src/handlers/airo.rs)); workflow step runs read the 10 newest ([workflows.rs](../src/handlers/workflows.rs)) and server-run office tasks read the 5 newest (`office_prompts` in [fsm_dispatcher.rs](../src/office/fsm_dispatcher.rs)). But chat replies, the daily plan and the kickoff **never read them**, and no screen lists them. The current-state research note said only workflows read lessons; the dispatcher code shows that server tasks do too. The biggest blind spot is the connections. There are 14 actions across Google, Stripe, Shopify and webhooks, but the server creates the bots, their keys "are never shown to anyone" ([mod.rs](../src/office/mod.rs)), and nothing on the chat reply path calls a connector. So the Finance Manager cannot see an open Stripe invoice and the Scheduler cannot see tomorrow's calendar, while inbound webhooks post a status note and nothing more ([connections.rs](../src/handlers/connections.rs)).

It is also hard to see what bots did. The dispatcher has explicit states, from QUEUED through WAITING_APPROVAL to COMPLETED or FAILED, but the web app picks one active task and shows it with an empty step list (lines 415–428 of [App.tsx](../web/src/App.tsx)). There is no task history, no per-bot spend and no in-app notification list; push covers approvals, and `/notifs` is a legacy server page. App blueprints define `quick_actions` and the docs define a `chart` view, yet neither renders ([ServerDrivenAppRenderer.tsx](../web/src/components/ServerDrivenAppRenderer.tsx)). Several things are missing outright. Contacts do not exist: people appear only as `name`, `client`, `customer` or `client_name` fields in four apps, and the only duplicate check is an exact match on a record's JSON hash ([architect.rs](../src/office/architect.rs)). There are no documents or uploads, and the router caps request bodies at 1 MB ([lib.rs](../src/lib.rs)). There is no full-text index, since `citext` is the only extension enabled ([0001_extensions.sql](../migrations/0001_extensions.sql)), and Ctrl/Cmd+K matches names only ([SearchPalette.tsx](../web/src/components/SearchPalette.tsx)). The owner also cannot delete a record or app, edit the website or focus after setup, invite teammates, or use the UI in another language. One bug matters directly for memory: the JSON branch of `GET /threads/:id` returns the **oldest** 500 notes (`ORDER BY seq ASC LIMIT 500`), and the web app reloads rooms through that branch, so once a room passes 500 messages a reload no longer shows its newest messages. I confirmed this by reading the code, not by running it.

## Twenty-eight additions, ranked and sequenced against today's code

The ranking weighs how much user evidence there is across both product categories, how big Airobot's gap is, and the effort. Trust features (what bots know, what they did and what it cost) come before autonomy features, because that is where complaints concentrate and where the market is heading. Effort is my own rough estimate for one developer, including the migration, the integration tests AGENTS.md requires for every endpoint, and the UI: **S** is up to a week, **M** one to three weeks and **L** four to eight weeks. "Now" means the next release, "Next" the one or two quarters after, and "Later" after that. Status describes the code as read for this research.

Most "Now" items connect code that already exists, so they pay off fast and the owner notices. Item 1 matters most, and the simplest compliant design skips free-form tool use. Instead, before the model call, the server deterministically fetches each area bot's standard read data: open invoices for Finance, upcoming events for Bookings, recent orders for Store, and recent matching Gmail threads for Sales and Support. It runs these fetches server-side under the office's own authorization, which sidesteps the hidden-key problem, and inserts the result as a short "What I can see right now" block. This follows Salesforce's lesson of pairing LLM reasoning with deterministic execution ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/is-salesforce-losing-confidence-in-llms/)). It also avoids multi-step agent loops, which by secondhand accounts of a Carnegie Mellon benchmark finish only about 24–34% of office tasks ([Creati.ai](https://creati.ai/ai-news/2026-02-04/microsoft-copilot-adoption-challenges/)). Connector data, like web pages today, has to be framed as information and never as instructions; Zenity Labs showed that a prompt injection in one Bedrock agent could reach other agents and plant false memories ([Zenity Labs](https://labs.zenity.io/post/agentcorruption-how-a-single-prompt-collapsed-the-entire-cloud-security-model)). Items 2 and 3 are each a few lines in `context::prompt` plus one screen, and they turn "the team learns" from a promise into something the owner can see. Item 6 builds on a meter that is already legible: a flat 25 credits per reply converts cleanly into "replies left today", which no credit-based competitor in the evidence offers.

"Next" introduces new data, and the order matters. Rolling summaries (#10) and contacts (#9) can be built in parallel, and contact-scoped memory needs both. The knowledge library (#12) and global search (#13) share the same full-text plumbing, so they should ship together. The proactive features (#15–#18) reuse the in-process loop pattern of the daily plan, so they need no scheduler or queue. "Later" holds the expensive channel and voice work. Three things should not be built at all: an automatic top-up, bots that insert themselves into every view, and a full CRM. Small businesses mostly want something close to a spreadsheet with a name, last contact date, next action and deal value ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/simplest-crm-small-business-start-with/)).

## Contacts, memory and documents all fit inside stock PostgreSQL 16

The constraints are tight. AGENTS.md rules out a search engine, vector database, cache or queue, and every environment runs plain PostgreSQL 16: `postgres:16` in Compose and CI, and embedded 16.16.0 on the desktop. Only `citext` is enabled. Migrations are append-only (the latest is `0054_connector_apps.sql`), and every table needs `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, soft delete, workspace authorization, an optional `idempotency_key` on writes, and an integration test per endpoint. All three features can share one row shape. Each item records its workspace and scope, a link to its source, whether an owner or a bot created it, whether it is pinned or when it expires, a `deleted_at`, and a `tsvector` column with a GIN index for search. With that shape, contacts, memory and documents become one "what the office knows" surface with one search, rather than three separate features.

### Contacts: one row per person, keyed on email, edited by suggestion

Every vendor in the evidence treats the person as the hub record, with a timeline of linked activity, and email is the de facto identity key. HubSpot blocks creating a contact whose email already exists ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/deduplication-of-records)), Folk merges duplicates by email for people and by domain for companies ([folk](https://www.folk.app/products/integrations)), and Shopify connectors upsert on email with phone as the fallback ([Zeotap](https://composable-docs.zeotap.com/destinations/ecommerce/shopify)). Airobot should normalize email (lowercased, trimmed and stored as `citext`, which is already installed) and make it unique per workspace among live rows. Phone should be a second match key but not a unique one, because families and front desks share numbers. External ids belong in `contact_identities`, so that re-importing Stripe customers updates records instead of duplicating them.

The failure to design against is the one consultancies report most often: duplicate records lead an agent to send one person two different emails, or to answer from the wrong record and find no order ([Campaign Creators](https://www.campaigncreators.com/blog/breeze-agents-run-on-data-here-s-what-breaks-hubspot-ai)). With messy data, agents produce poor results "just faster and at a bigger scale" ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/why-ai-agents-fail-in-messy-crms-a-four-layer-readiness-test-for-revenue-teams)). Airobot's bots already write records through `ADD <app> {json}` lines ([context.rs](../src/office/context.rs)), so the duplicate check belongs in that write path, before the insert. When a record carries an email or phone, the server looks up the contact and links it through `office_records.contact_id`; if nothing matches, it creates a contact with `source = 'bot'`. A bot never overwrites a field on an existing contact. A changed phone or address becomes an approval ("Update Maria's phone to …?"), the same suggest-then-approve model Attio uses ([Attio](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-ai/ask-attio)). The timeline should be a query over linked rows rather than a copied events table, which matches how HubSpot presents activities ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-activity-properties)). It pulls the contact's records, memory items and contact-linked conversations, and keeps `last_contacted_at` and `last_activity_at` as roll-ups.

Marketing consent gets its own field in Shopify's shape, a marketing state with an opt-in level and a timestamp ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/mutations/customerEmailMarketingConsentUpdate)), not a tag. Bots check it before any marketing message, which today guards a capability that does not yet exist. A CSV upload fits under the 1 MB limit for a few thousand rows, and Stripe customers can come in through the existing `stripe.customers` action. Shopify customers and Google People need new connector actions, since neither is in the catalog today. The screen should be the existing datatable renderer with few columns, not a CRM. A duplicate-merge tool that soft-deletes the losing record and repoints its links can follow later. Fuzzy name matching needs `pg_trgm`, which ships with standard PostgreSQL contrib, but the researchers did not verify it in the official image or the desktop build, so version 1 should not depend on it; a `TODO(air):` should name that tradeoff.

### Memory: four scopes, one rolling summary, nothing saved silently

The major assistants have settled on the same pattern. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft 365 Copilot each offer saved memories or a summary the user can view and edit, memory scoped to a project, a temporary or incognito chat, and admin off-switches ([Claude](https://claude.com/blog/memory); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/Microsoft-365-Copilot/manage-copilot-memory-in-microsoft-365-copilot)). Business agents add per-customer memory: Intercom keeps Fin's recent summaries for **45 days and facts for 180** ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/16001516-fin-memory)), and Salesforce keeps an episodic summary per conversation plus semantic facts tied to a resolved individual ([Salesforce](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/data/agentic-memory-context/guide/agentic-memory-about.html)). The user complaints are just as consistent. Memory bleeds from one client into another ([Intelligence by Intent](https://intelligencebyintent.com/insights/he-turned-off-chatgpts-memory-it-referenced-another-client-anyway)), the UI gives no warning when memory is full while claiming it remembered ([OpenAI Community](https://community.openai.com/t/chatgpt-plus-plan-memory-issue-no-warning-when-memory-is-full-leading-to-lost-entries/1111613)), deleted memories come back ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2025/10/01/chatgpt-users-cant-delete-memory/)), and memory gets written without being asked: one study of 2,050 ChatGPT memories found **96% were triggered by the model, not the user** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01450v2)). The implementation guidance points the same way: "do the simplest thing that works", which means compacting the conversation into a summary and keeping structured notes outside the context window ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)). Lindy went further and replaced its RAG stack with plain files that it rebuilds every 12 hours ([Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/engineering/memory-agent)).

For Airobot, the "sessions" are its threads; the `sessions` table holds sign-in cookies and is unrelated. When a room passes 20 notes beyond `summary_through_seq`, an in-process database loop like the one that runs the daily plan rewrites `threads.summary` from the old summary plus the new notes, with a `TODO(air):` naming how often. The prompt then holds, in order, the bot's character, the stored business profile, the lessons, office and bot memory items, the room summary, the last 20 notes, and that contact's memory when the conversation is linked to one. Summaries are best effort, so a failed summary must never block a reply, as practitioners recommend ([aalok05](https://aalok05.github.io/ai/2026/03/12/agentic-session-management.html)). The same summary drives a "where we left off" card on the room, and the Architect's prompt gets one line per active room, which closes the "no idea what it was doing in another channel" gap (#11).

Memory items come from two places. When the owner says "remember this", the item is saved directly. A bot can propose a `REMEMBER:` line, parsed like the existing `ADD` and `Question:` lines, and each such item is saved with a visible "Remembered: … Undo" chip in the chat, because a silent, model-made memory is the top trust breaker. Bot output must be validated strictly and malformed lines dropped; one 2026 study reports about 25% malformed JSON from one extraction setup ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15903)). A contradicted fact gets an `invalidated_at` instead of being deleted, following Zep's timestamped invalidation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956)) and the project's soft-delete rule. Unpinned summaries expire after about 45 days and unpinned facts after about 180, following Fin, so the store stays bounded without a capacity cap that fails silently.

Contact memory loads only when a conversation is explicitly tied to that person: a chat opened from the contact page, a record in the turn linked to them, or an exact email or phone match. It never loads because a name merely looks similar, and that rule is the structural defense against memory bleed. The owner controls memory through plain lists on the office, bot and contact pages, where each item has edit, delete and pin plus a "from conversation on …" source link. There is also an off-the-record switch per room (`threads.memory_off`), an office-wide memory switch, and separate "delete memory" and "delete conversation" actions, since ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini all confuse users by quietly decoupling the two. Retrieval needs no vectors: office and bot items are few enough to include in full, and older contact facts can be ranked with the same `tsvector` search used for documents. Two costs need a `TODO(air):`. A summary is an extra AI call, metered in the cloud and free in local mode, and under the flat 25-credit price, longer prompts cost the operator rather than the owner.

### Knowledge: full-text retrieval and real citations before any vectors

Every comparable product offers the same three ways to add knowledge (file upload, a website or URL crawl, and connector sync), and they differ in scoping, freshness and how much they show about what was retrieved. The dominant user complaint is silent failure. Users report GPTs "ignoring attached knowledge document making up stuff" ([OpenAI Community](https://community.openai.com/t/gpt-ignoring-attached-knowledge-document-making-up-stuff/865764)), fabricated citations ([OpenAI Community](https://community.openai.com/t/is-chatgpt-hallucinating-details-about-your-files-too/1167197)), and a Chatbase bot that "acts like it's not aware of some of the data that is loaded into it" ([AppSumo](https://appsumo.com/products/chatbase/reviews/isnt-working-correctly-no-support-251372/)). The fix users find is visibility: Copilot Studio users are pointed to a test pane that shows the retrieved knowledge ([Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/microsoft365copilot/questions-about-copilot-agents-created-using-copilot-studio-that-use-sharepoint-/4429430)). For scoping, Airobot should use an office-level library that every bot reads by default, as all of Sintra's helpers share one Brain AI ([Sintra](https://help.sintra.ai/en/articles/12266276-brain-ai-explained)), and let owners narrow a bot to selected documents through `bot_documents`. That covers both the "shared brain" and the per-bot cases.

Version 1 needs no new extension. `document_chunks` holds the content plus a heading or page reference, and its `tsvector` column weights the title above the body, with a GIN index beside a btree on `workspace_id`. Queries use `websearch_to_tsquery`, which is safe for untrusted input, and `ts_rank_cd`, which rewards terms that appear close together ([PostgreSQL docs](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/10/textsearch-controls.html)). A stored generated column must pin one text-search configuration, so version 1 should use `simple` or compute the vector at write time from a per-office language setting. Retrieval takes the top 20 chunks, since Anthropic found that 20 beat 5 and 10 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). When a bot's attached library is small, retrieval is skipped and the library goes in whole, as Claude Projects and Relevance AI do ([Claude help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11473015-retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-for-projects); [Relevance AI](https://relevanceai.com/docs/build/knowledge/access-knowledge)). Anthropic puts the no-RAG line at 200,000 tokens, but under the flat per-call cloud price Airobot needs a much lower per-bot budget, which deserves a `TODO(air):`.

Citations should be correct by construction. Retrieved chunks go into the prompt as numbered sources, and the server keeps only the citation numbers that match a retrieved chunk id, rendering them as chips that show the document plus its heading or page. A model-written URL never becomes a link. That structurally prevents the fabricated citations users complain about, and it is the test vendors themselves recommend ([Wonderchat](https://wonderchat.io/blog/ai-chatbot-source-citations)). Around that core sit the trust features users ask for by name. A per-bot "only from documents" switch answers "that's not in my documents" and offers to create a task for the owner. A "what this bot knows" page lists each source with its status, last-indexed time and chunk count, and a "try a question" panel shows the top chunks for a query. Any cited answer gets a "mark outdated" action. Documents are private by default, with only `public` sources citable to outside visitors later; HubSpot's rule that "private sources won't be cited, but their content may still appear in responses" is a leak to avoid ([HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/customer-agent/manage-the-customer-agents-content-sources?LanguageId=1)).

Ingestion should start with what Airobot already touches: website pages (the kickoff already reads the site), pasted FAQs and TXT/MD files. DOCX, through `docx-lite` or a small zip plus XML reader, and PDF, through `pdf-extract` or `pdf_oxide`, follow ([docx-lite](https://docs.rs/crate/docx-lite/latest); [pdf-extract](https://docs.rs/pdf-extract/0.7.6/index.html)). The upload route alone should get a larger body limit, around the 10–20 MB Sintra allows ([Sintra](https://help.sintra.ai/en/articles/11904647-how-do-i-use-the-add-button-to-upload-files-to-brain-ai)), while the global 1 MB limit stays. Originals go into a capped `document_blobs` table so documents can be re-chunked later and backups stay one database, with a `TODO(air):` on database size. Extraction runs inside `spawn_blocking` with a timeout and a panic guard. The text is normalized (NFKC, collapsed whitespace, repeated headers removed) and chunked into a few hundred tokens along headings. PDFs with no text layer are marked "needs OCR" instead of silently indexing nothing ([DEV](https://dev.to/iterationlayer/the-hidden-failure-modes-of-pdf-processing-2kmh)). CSV files are imported as records or contacts rather than chunked, following Salesforce's guidance to load structured data as structured data ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/agentforce-and-rag/)). Document text, like web pages, is information and never instructions.

Version 2 adds semantic recall only where it is cheap to run. It means switching Compose and CI to the `pgvector/pgvector` image, bundling the extension for the desktop's embedded Postgres on macOS and Windows, and asking self-hosters on managed Postgres to enable it ([pgvector](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector)). Embeddings are computed only when the office has a key or a local model: OpenAI's `text-embedding-3-small` costs $0.02 per million tokens ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/text-embedding-3-small)), and Ollama or `fastembed-rs` run locally ([Ollama](https://docs.ollama.com/api/embed); [fastembed-rs](https://github.com/Anush008/fastembed-rs)). Simple arithmetic puts 20,000 chunks at about $0.20 to embed once, so money is not the constraint; operational complexity is. Vectors go in a table keyed by model and are searched by exact scan within the workspace, because an approximate index can drop matches after filtering. They are combined with full-text results by reciprocal rank fusion with k = 60 in one SQL statement ([AlloyDB](https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/ai/run-hybrid-vector-similarity-search?authuser=0)). A possible alternative needs no extension at all: store small embeddings as `real[]` and rank them in Rust for offices under a few thousand chunks. That is my untested inference, to be benchmarked before anyone relies on it. Version 3 adds two optional quality steps: prepending the document title and section path to each chunk before indexing, and reranking the top results with the office's own model.

## Kiro Crew is AWS's bot workspace, and its goal cards transfer directly

The match is close to exact. **Kiro Crew** is the Kiro team's open-source, Apache-2.0 "persistent workspace for development work that self-improves and continues beyond one session" ([GitHub](https://github.com/kirodotdev/kirocrew)). It runs on the Kiro CLI through the Agent Client Protocol ([aimadetools](https://www.aimadetools.com/blog/kiro-crew-complete-guide/)), and AWS made its **Bots** generally available on 5 November 2026 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/aws-launches-agentic-ai-kiro-bots-with-memory-and-support-for-recurring-development-jobs)). The agents were first called "crewmates", then "Mates", and are now "Bots" ([Crew changelog](https://kiro.dev/changelog/crew/)), a history that could explain a fuzzy memory of the name. No AI product called "Kiri" turned up. The fallbacks rank well below it: "Kiro: Sales AI ChatBot Agent" is an unrelated Shopify app that answers shoppers' questions about products and orders ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/kiro)), CrewAI is a Python framework built on role-playing "Crews" and event-driven "Flows" ([GitHub](https://github.com/crewAIInc/crewAI)), and Kroolo's "Kroo AI" is pitched as "your new AI Team" ([Kroolo](https://corp.kroolo.com/ai/ai-agent)). One question to the user settles it: "the AWS one with bots in Slack or Discord, or a Shopify sales chatbot?"

Kiro Crew is useful because each bot is one long-lived contact rather than a chat session. A bot keeps a single conversation with you, its own memory and its own workspace; it handles short tasks itself and hands long ones to background workers that report back while you keep chatting ([How bots work](https://kiro.dev/docs/crew/bots/how-bots-work/)). After you approve a goal, the bot proposes measurable milestones. Check-ins run daily, weekly or monthly but are off by default, the weekly report is saved as a document, and every step arrives as a card you confirm ([Bots docs](https://kiro.dev/docs/crew/bots/); [Crew 0.10 changelog](https://kiro.dev/changelog/crew/0-10/)). Memory, lessons and synthesized skills stay visible and editable ([Crew docs](https://kiro.dev/docs/crew/)). Every dashboard figure is read from the bot's log, and values the bot wrote itself are tagged as such ([Crew changelog](https://kiro.dev/changelog/crew/)). You can reach the same bot from Slack, Discord, Telegram, Teams and other apps without moving its state, and pick a model per bot ([Crew docs](https://kiro.dev/docs/crew/); [Introducing bots](https://kiro.dev/blog/crew-bots/)).

The criticisms are just as instructive. **New bots start in Trust mode**, which acts without approving each action, and Crew's default sandbox leaves `~/.ssh` visible to agent commands ([DEV](https://dev.to/gabrielanhaia/awss-kiro-bots-start-in-trust-mode-and-crews-default-sandbox-leaves-ssh-visible-539f)). "Open source" means "free to read, not free to run", because every agent request spends Kiro credits ([makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/kiro-crew-review/)). And on a pre-release build, a bot went idle after its milestones were approved until the user nudged it ([DEV](https://dev.to/aws/understanding-an-unfamiliar-codebase-with-kiro-crew-bots-1gkg)).

And Kiro bots draw on the same credit pool as interactive work ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/aws-kiro-bots-ga-kiro-crew-scheduled-coding-agents-pooled-credits-permissions-memory-team-admin-limits)), whereas Airobot should show scheduled spend separately for each bot. Airobot's kickoff already avoids Kiro's idle-after-approval problem, because its bots start their first task immediately.

## The evidence is thinnest exactly where owners speak plainly

The research could not reach the most direct small-business voices. **Reddit was unreachable**, because the search tool refuses reddit.com and page fetches were blocked, so r/smallbusiness, r/AI_Agents and similar communities appear only secondhand, quoted by competitor blogs such as Lindy's Viktor review and Oliv.ai's analysis of Agentforce reviews. Page fetches also failed for G2, Trustpilot and many vendor documentation hosts, including help.openai.com, intercom.com, attio.com, lindy.ai, learn.microsoft.com, developer.salesforce.com, kiro.dev, dev.to and aws.amazon.com. **Most quotes in this report therefore come from search-engine excerpts rather than full pages**, and they should be spot-checked before anyone republishes them. Only a few pages were read in full, among them Anthropic's memory and context-engineering posts and the Claude Projects help article.

The sources also lean in known directions. Many "reviews" in the AI-employee category come from affiliates or competitors: Lindy's blog reviews Sintra and Viktor, Marblism's blog reviews Sintra alternatives, and eesel, Carly, Macha and Pluno publish many "X review" pages. Trustpilot draws billing complaints while G2 and the app stores draw satisfied users, so neither alone describes a typical experience. No source gives complaint frequencies, so the theme rankings here come from the researchers' reading of the excerpts, and price figures conflict across sources and change often. The memory evidence comes mostly from consumer ChatGPT users rather than small businesses buying agents, and the retrieval benchmarks are vendor numbers or Anthropic's 2024 datasets, not small-business document sets. On the Airobot side, the capability map comes from reading the code rather than running the app. I spot-checked its central claims against the source; that check corrected one claim, since server tasks do read lessons, and confirmed the reload issue, though only by reading the code. ## Conclusion

The market's 2025–2026 retreat from autonomy suggests Airobot should compete on legibility rather than capability. Artisan hired a human sales rep, Lindy became an assistant you text, and Salesforce moved toward deterministic execution; in that market, the products that keep trust let an owner see what the bot knows, what it did and what it cost. Airobot can build all three as rows in its one PostgreSQL database, which turns the "no extra infrastructure" rule from a handicap into a feature. When the business profile, lessons, contacts, memory, documents, task outcomes and spend all live in one place, the owner can inspect, search, edit and audit every bit of it, something a product built on a vector store, a queue and a separate CRM cannot easily offer.

The cheapest and largest win is the least glamorous one: connect what is already built. The lessons table, the kickoff summary and the 14 connector actions all exist, but chat never uses them. An owner who rejects an approval with a reason, or connects Stripe, reasonably believes the team now knows. Until the chat reply path reads those parts, every new feature sits on bots that look forgetful and blind, which is exactly the complaint that sinks the competitors.
